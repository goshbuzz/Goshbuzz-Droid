# Code of conduct

This is a privacy project. The people who need it most are often the people with the least room for error:
journalists, activists, abuse survivors, and ordinary users who cannot afford to be tracked. That shapes how
we treat each other here.

## Expected

- **Be honest, especially about limits.** "This does not protect you from X" is a contribution, not negativity.
- **Be patient with beginners.** Most people have never built a ROM. There are no stupid questions, only
  unhelpful answers.
- **Criticise the work, not the person.** Technical disagreement is welcome and should be specific.
- **Respect privacy of others.** Do not publish another contributor's personal details, device identifiers,
  or private messages.
- **Assume good faith, then verify facts.** Ask for a source rather than calling something wrong.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, threats, insults, or sustained disruption.
- Discriminatory comments about race, ethnicity, religion, gender, sexuality, disability, age, or nationality.
- Publishing someone's private information.
- Pressuring anyone to test risky changes on hardware they cannot afford to lose.
- Helping to build surveillance, tracking, or attack capability against people who have not consented.
- Advocating for bypassing protections on devices the person does not own.

## Scope

This applies in project spaces (repository, issues, pull requests, chat, calls) and when representing the
project in public.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report concerns privately to the maintainer through the project's published contact channel. Reports are
handled confidentially. The maintainer may warn, remove content, or block participation. Because the
maintainer is currently one person, serious reports involving the maintainer should be raised with a
second maintainer once the project has one; this limitation is stated openly rather than hidden.

## Attribution

This is a short, project-specific code of conduct written for GoshBuzz OS rather than an adopted template,
so that its wording about honesty and hardware risk matches the project's actual rules.
